Create a PDF from Images: Designer, Templates, Watermark and Password Protection

How-to October 11, 2026 · 10 min read

Photos for a client, a portfolio for a job application, the pictures from a site inspection: many images often need to go out as one single, neatly designed document. The KodiniTools batch editor exports your image series directly as a PDF. You choose the page size and layout, add a header and footer, a watermark and, if you like, a password. In the built-in PDF designer you create a cover page and as many comment pages as you need, with texts, images, shapes and templates. Everything runs in your browser — no installation, no sign-up, no upload.

Step by step: export images as a PDF

  1. Open the image series editor

    Go to kodinitools.com/bilderseriebearbeiten — free, no sign-up.

  2. Load images

    Load files via drag & drop, “Select Files” or “Select Folder”. To export only some of them, select the images you want in the gallery.

  3. Open the PDF export

    In the status bar, click the PDF icon and choose “Export all images as PDF” or “Export selection as PDF”. The PDF settings open.

  4. Set page and layout

    Choose orientation, page size, page margin, images per page and image fit. Optionally add a header and footer, page numbers, bookmarks, a watermark, a password and the author.

  5. Design the cover and comment pages

    Enable “Design Custom Front Page” and click “Design Front Page”. For extra pages, enable “Add Comment Page” and open “Design Comment Page”. “Save” keeps your layout.

  6. Export or print

    “Export” creates the PDF in your browser and saves it. “Print” creates the PDF and sends it straight to this device's printer. A progress bar shows how far along it is, and you can cancel the export at any time.

Page size and layout

Besides A4 you can choose A5, A3, US Letter and a 210 × 210 mm square, for example for photo books. Under “Custom” you enter width and height in millimeters, each from 50 to 1000 mm. The page margin ranges from 0 to 40 mm, with 10 mm as the default. Cover and comment pages are scaled proportionally to every size, so your layout stays intact.

Each page holds 1, 2, 4, 6 or 9 images. That turns 90 photos into a 10-page contact sheet instead of a 90-page PDF. The image fit decides how an image fills its cell:

  • Fit (whole image): the whole image is shown, empty areas stay white.
  • Fill (edges cropped): the image fills the cell completely, overhanging edges are cropped. Ideal for a calm, even grid.

Image comments instead of file names

The single image editor has an “Image comment (PDF)” field. Whatever you enter there appears centered below the image in the PDF, across several lines if needed and also in grids with several images per page. File names like “IMG_4711.jpg” no longer show up in the PDF. The comment is also included when you download a PDF straight from the single image editor.

Header, footer, page numbers and bookmarks

Header and footer each take up to 120 characters and understand placeholders that are replaced on export:

  • {page} and {total}: current page and total, e.g. “Page {page} of {total}”.
  • {title}: the document title, “Image collection” by default.
  • {date}: the export date.

“Show page numbers” numbers the pages automatically. The cover page never gets a header or footer. “Create bookmarks” is on by default and adds a table of contents in the PDF viewer: the cover, every comment page and every image, with the first line of its comment added to each image.

Watermark in the PDF

The PDF watermark uses the same controls as the image watermark: text, font, font size, bold and italic, color, opacity, rotation and position. You can choose center, the four corners and “Tile (repeated)”, which spreads the text across the whole page. The default is a subtle gray at 25 % opacity. You can also upload a logo. It takes up a quarter of the page width, keeps its transparency as a PNG and is not rotated. The watermark appears on every page of the PDF.

Password protection and printing rights

With “Protect PDF with a password”, the document can only be opened with the password. Use 1 to 32 characters, ASCII only (no umlauts or emoji). “Allow printing” decides whether recipients may print the PDF. An invalid password blocks the export instead of silently creating an unprotected PDF. The password is neither stored nor logged.

Good to know: The tool uses standard PDF encryption (RC4, 40-bit), which every PDF viewer can open. It prevents casual opening, for example of a forwarded email, but not targeted attacks. Write the password down: without it, the PDF cannot be opened.

Print directly

Next to “Export” there is a “Print” button. It creates the PDF and prints it on the device you are using. The route adapts to the device: in a desktop browser the print dialog opens right away, on iPhone and iPad the PDF opens in a new tab and prints via “Share → Print”. Where the browser cannot display PDFs, as on many Android devices, the PDF is saved and you print it from the file. Printing skips the password protection, because the PDF never leaves your device.

The PDF designer for cover and comment pages

Cover and comment pages are made in the same designer. The cover is a single page, comment pages can be as many as you like. Without a designed cover, the tool automatically creates a simple title page with title, image count, date and author.

Text with rich text, fonts and placeholders

  • 14 fonts: including Helvetica, Clash Display, General Sans, Hind, Satoshi, Tanker and Zodiak. The fonts are embedded in the PDF, so the text stays real, searchable and copyable text.
  • Size, color, alignment: 10 to 72 px, any color, left, centered or right, opacity per element.
  • Bold and italic inside a text: select single words and press Ctrl+B or Ctrl+I, or type **bold** and *italic* directly. To write an asterisk itself, use \*.
  • Placeholders: {title}, {date}, {images} and {author} are replaced with title, date, image count and author on export, so one template fits every export.
  • Edit in place: double-click a text on the canvas.

Images, shapes and page background

  • Images: upload a logo, cover photo or signature and set size and position freely. Opacity is carried over into the PDF.
  • Shapes: rectangle, circle or ellipse and line, with fill color or no fill, stroke color, stroke width, corner radius and opacity. Shapes end up as vectors in the PDF and stay sharp at any zoom level.
  • Page background: a color or an image across the whole page.
  • Layers: use “Front” and “Back” to place text on top of an image or shape.

Templates

Five templates are built in: Photo book, Report and Portfolio for the cover, Note and Two columns for comment pages. A template fills the page with ready-arranged elements that you can then adjust freely. Save your own layouts as named templates in your browser. With “Export templates” and “Import templates” you take them to another computer as a file or share them with your team.

Work precisely

  • Undo and redo: Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y or the buttons in the sidebar.
  • Guides and snapping: elements snap to page edges, the page center and other elements. Hold Alt to drag freely.
  • Multi-select: Shift+click or Ctrl+A, then move, duplicate (Ctrl+D), copy and paste (Ctrl+C/V) or delete together. Arrow keys move in small steps.
  • Arrange: align left, right, top, bottom or centered, to the page or to the selection. Distribute evenly horizontally or vertically from three elements.
  • Manage pages: reorder comment pages as thumbnails by dragging, duplicate or delete them.
  • Preview: shows the finished page without frames and handles.

Structure of the finished PDF

The PDF always follows the same structure: first the cover or the automatic title page, then the comment pages, then the images in the chosen layout. Title, author and language are also stored in the PDF's document properties. Every image is scaled to at most 1920 px on the longest side and embedded as JPEG; transparent PNGs get a white background. Images are prepared in parallel in the background, so the page stays responsive even with large series.

Tip for a contact sheet: landscape, 6 images per page, “Fill” and a 5 mm margin. Put “Page {page} of {total} · {date}” in the footer and use the “Portfolio” template as the cover. In a minute you have a document that works well for letting clients pick their images.

Typical use cases

  • Photographers: send a selection from a shoot to clients as a contact sheet with a cover, logo watermark and password.
  • Applications and portfolios: work samples with a designed cover, descriptions on comment pages and bookmarks for quick navigation.
  • Trades and construction: photos of an inspection or damage report with image comments, page numbers and the date in the footer.
  • Real estate: property photos with a cover image, short description and company logo as a simple brochure.
  • Personal: vacation photos in a square format as a photo book PDF, printed straight on your home printer.

Privacy first

The PDF is created entirely in your browser. Images, texts, logos and passwords are processed locally on your device and are not uploaded to a server. Your own templates live only in your browser's storage. Confidential material such as client photos or damage reports stays with you.
